1Byline under the H1

Where
Inside the hero (city-hero-filmstrip), directly under the H1 "Can I See a Blue Staffy Puppy on Video From London Before Any Deposit?".
Says
"Written by Lisa Bright, breeder, Carlisle", with her name linking to /blue-staffy-uk-breeders/. Once her read is recorded, a second line: "Read and approved by Lisa on <date>". Before that, the second line is not printed at all. Each frame shows both states.
No headshot
We have no real photo of Lisa on file, and Google's guidance warns against made-up author photos, so none of the three uses a picture.
Note
The hero's lead paragraph already ends "Lisa Bright, BlueStaffyUK." With a byline in place, that sign-off says the same thing twice. Dropping it would be a content change, so it is flagged here, not made.

ASigned rule Recommended

Why: it is the smallest change to the approved hero: one line under the H1 with a short brass rule, no box, and the "read" line slots in under it without moving the lead or the filmstrip. Trade-off: it is the quietest of the three, so the "read and approved" line is easy to miss.

BLedger pair

Two labelled cells between hairlines: "Written by" and, once recorded, "Read and approved". They sit side by side when the column is wide enough and stack when it is not. Clear labels, but it adds a ruled band to a hero that is already dense.

CMonogram seal

A lettered "LB" seal (type only, no photo) beside the byline; the seal fills brass once her read is recorded. It has the most presence, but a round mark where a face usually goes can read as a stand-in for a photo, which is the impression the no-headshot rule is there to avoid.

2Puppy strip under the key takeaways

Where
Straight after the city-takeaways-ledger ("Key Takeaways for London Buyers"), in the content column beside the page dial. No new heading, so the approved outline does not change.
Data
The six puppies in data/puppies.json: name, sex, colour, price_gbp. Each links to /available-puppies/<slug>/, the route src/pages/available-puppies/[slug].astro builds (all six are in dist/available-puppies/). The build reads the prices from the data file. They are typed into this preview only so you can see them, and each one is marked in a comment as interpolated.
Refresh delta
The page already shows these six puppies three ways, all with photos: the hero filmstrip, the city-puppy-sheet prints and the city-roster table. None of the three strips below uses a photo.

ATicket strip Recommended

Why: each ticket is one link to that puppy's own page, and with no photo it does not repeat the six faces (and their alts, rule 11) that the hero filmstrip shows one screen up. On desktop it stays one row tall. Trade-off: without a picture or a colour key, the coats can't be told apart at a glance.

BCoat swatch rail

Each puppy gets a round colour key painted from the colour on its listing (blue, white, blue and white, blue with a white blaze), using our own steel and bone. On desktop it is a ruled rail of six, on a phone a list. It is the quickest way to see the coats, but a flat swatch is not the dog, and a buyer could read it as a promise about the exact shade.

CBoys and girls ledger

Two short tables, boys and girls, because that is how the price is set (male_gbp / female_gbp). They stack into labelled rows below 640px (.stack-table, rule 13). This shows the clearest reason for the price difference, but it repeats the boy/girl split the price scale and the roster already make.

3Video-call checklist

Where
deposit-viewing, chapter 1, under the H5 "What Should I Ask to See While We Are on the Call?" and its paragraph. In city-chapters that prose column is only about 260px wide at 1280, so the checklist runs across the chapter's full width beneath the paragraph.
Items
Eight, each from a data file or one of Lisa's answers (named in an HTML comment on each item): Maggie on camera (q03), your puppy's markings against its listing (puppies.json), the paperwork and the contract on camera (faq.json whyus-paperwork, q05), the tests named only, with no results (q01), the parents' registration numbers, the deposit with deposit_refund_clause read out (q04 bank transfer), and the guarantee from guarantee_label / guarantee_cover / guarantee_note.
Mechanics
A native <input type="checkbox"> inside its <label>, with no JavaScript. A ticked item gets a brass strike-through through CSS :has(). Nothing is saved: the boxes are for use during the call.

ACall sheet

One white sheet under a brass clip, with "On camera" and "Out loud" as small group labels and a torn-off foot. It reads like something to keep beside the phone. As a single column it is the tallest of the three on desktop.

BLook and listen Recommended

Why: it splits the call into the two things a video call can prove: what you see on camera (Maggie, the puppy, the papers: q03, q05) and what you hear us say (the tests named, the deposit by bank transfer and its refund clause: q01, q04). The two panes sit side by side from 640px, so on desktop it is half the height of A. Trade-off: on a phone the two panes stack, so it ends up a little taller than one plain list.

CCall window

The checklist inside a dark video-call window, with a "Live call with Lisa" bar and two columns. It is the most memorable of the three and ties straight to the H1's promise. The cost: a fourth steel-900 block on a page that already has the hero and the dark bands, and dark text-heavy panels are harder to scan.

4London places under the parks H4

Where
london-life, chapter 1, under the H4 "Where Can a Flat-Dwelling Staffy Stretch Its Legs in London's Parks?" and its paragraph, across the chapter's full width.
Data
data/city-places/blue-staffy-puppies-london.json (fetched 2026-10-03): ten places, their dog-rule facts as the file words them, and each fact's official source. Then one line linking the RCVS Find a Vet search (vets.source). Nothing else.
Left out on purpose
Facts in the file that are not about dogs: park sizes, Trent Park's toilets, Oaks Park's postal address. The file's general rules list (microchip law, the Royal Parks' rules for every park, Enfield's PSPO 6) is also left out, as the brief asked: places only. No borough is shown for the Royal Parks or Epping Forest, because the sources don't name one (marked NOT FETCHED in the file).
Chips
"No dogs", "On a lead" and "Good to know" sort each fact as its wording reads. The words themselves are the file's.

ARule cards

One white card per place, two across from 640px, each with its own source link at the foot. Easy to scan by park, but the Royal Parks PDF is linked five times with the same anchor, and ten cards make the tallest block of the three.

BRules table

One real table: place, no dogs, on a lead, good to know, source. It stacks into labelled rows below 640px (.stack-table, rule 13). It is the densest and easiest to compare across parks. The cells shorten the file's sentences into lists (the places themselves are kept word for word), and that shortening would have to be checked against each source.

CGrouped by who sets the rules Recommended

Why: each publisher's page is linked once, in the group's header, so the Royal Parks PDF covers its five parks in one link. That takes the block from 12 links to 8 on the board (rule 12), and a buyer can see whose rules they are reading. The file's full sentences stay as they are. Trade-off: someone hunting for one park has to scan four groups instead of an A–Z list.

5New links for the board

Every link the four pieces add, plus one already on the page that the board's link list misses. Rule 12: a link that isn't on the approved board isn't built. Anchors are the ones the recommended styles use.
